# The First Week, Handled

*A printable, fillable checklist for your first week at a new job. Print it, pin it, cross things off. That's the whole system.*

From **Asdale Dynamics, LLC** — plain-English life-admin tools made for real humans (ADHD-friendly: short steps, no judgment).

---

## How to use this

- Print it, or open the HTML version in your browser and type right into the boxes, then hit Print.
- You do not need to finish everything in week one. Deadlines matter; niceness can wait.
- If your week goes sideways (it probably will), the only must-dos are marked with a star (★).

---

## Before Day 1

- [ ] ★ Confirm start time, building/entrance, and who to ask for when you arrive
- [ ] ★ Confirm dress code (ask your contact directly: "what do people actually wear?")
- [ ] Check how you'll get there and do a dry run if the commute is new
- [ ] Pack the paperwork you were told to bring (ID, tax forms, banking info)
- [ ] Locate the offer letter and save a copy somewhere you can find it
- [ ] Set two alarms. Then one backup alarm.

## Day 1

- [ ] ★ Arrive a few minutes early — early is on time for week one
- [ ] Meet whoever picks you up / reception; get a badge or visitor pass
- [ ] Say your name a lot. People expect it. You don't have to be smooth.
- [ ] ★ Complete onboarding paperwork (see the W-4 & Direct Deposit checklist)
- [ ] Get access basics working: computer login, email, badge door
- [ ] Learn the basics: where's the bathroom, where's lunch, when's lunch
- [ ] Write down 2–3 names of people you met (spelling matters later)
- [ ] Save or screenshot IT help-desk contact info — you will need it

## Day 2

- [ ] ★ Finish any remaining onboarding forms (don't let tax/bank forms linger)
- [ ] Set up remaining systems: chat/teams app, phone, voicemail greeting
- [ ] Ask for your team's calendar and request the standing meetings
- [ ] Learn the org shape: your manager, your manager's boss, your teammates
- [ ] Start a "what I'd tell past-me" note — jot down jargon as you hear it

## Day 3

- [ ] ★ Find out the benefits enrollment deadline (see the Deadline Tracker)
- [ ] Read the employee handbook at normal speed — skim is fine
- [ ] Ask your manager: "What does success in my first 30 days look like?"
- [ ] Learn time-off basics: how to request PTO, who approves it
- [ ] Eat lunch with someone if you can. It counts as work.

## Day 4

- [ ] ★ Submit benefits elections if the deadline is near (see Deadline Tracker)
- [ ] Double-check your first paycheck details (see W-4 & Direct Deposit checklist)
- [ ] Ask a teammate what "normal" looks like: start time, end time, quiet hours
- [ ] Say yes to one small task you can complete and be seen doing

## Day 5

- [ ] ★ Send a short end-of-week note to your manager: what you did, one question
- [ ] Put recurring meetings on your calendar so they stop surprising you
- [ ] Scan/photograph every paper you signed this week and file it (see Papers: Keep or Shred)
- [ ] Put your badge somewhere specific tonight so it's the same place every day
- [ ] Do something nice for yourself this weekend. You did a real thing.

---

## If you do only three things this week

1. Turn in the W-4 and direct deposit forms — wrong info here is slow and annoying to fix.
2. Find out the benefits enrollment deadline in writing.
3. Leave Friday knowing what's expected of you next week.

Anything else can spill into week two. Nobody is keeping score except you.
