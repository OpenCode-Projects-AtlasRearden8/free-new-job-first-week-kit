# Badge Photo Tips (First Week Photo Checklist)

*New-job week often includes a photo for your ID badge. It might be taken on-site in about 20 seconds, or you might be asked to submit one. Here's how to not spend three years looking at a photo you hate.*

From **Asdale Dynamics, LLC** — warm, plain-English, zero-judgment. Photo tips, not vanity.

---

## The 60-second recipe (works with any phone)

1. **Light in front of you**, not behind. Face a window (this one tip does 80% of the work). Avoid standing *under* hard ceiling lights.
2. **Plain background.** A blank wall beats a busy one. If the badge system crops to a headshot anyway, plain still looks better.
3. **Camera at eye level or slightly above.** Held low, even slightly, adds chin and subtracts confidence.
4. **Frame head + shoulders.** The system will crop it; give it something generous to crop from.
5. **Neutral, friendly expression.** A slight smile beats both the forced grin and the "perp walk" face.
6. **Take 5, pick 1.** Not 50. Five is enough — you have paperwork to do.

## Quick pre-shot checklist

- [ ] Face a window, sun in front of you (not overhead, not behind)
- [ ] Plain wall behind you
- [ ] Camera at eye level
- [ ] Glasses: fine to wear — tilt slightly down if glare appears
- [ ] Wipe the lens (yes, really — half of "bad phone cameras" are smudgy ones)
- [ ] Collar/hair: whatever you'd wear to a normal workday, not a special occasion
- [ ] Look *at* the lens, not at the screen image of yourself

## If they take the photo on-site

- Ask if you can see it before it's saved. Most people won't ask. You may. Worst case: "any chance of a redo?"; it costs them 15 seconds.
- If the photo is awful and permanent-feeling: badge reprints are typically cheap or free — it's a known, normal office request.
- Stand still, shoulders level, look over the photographer's ear — not the lens they're holding.

## If you submit your own photo

- Check dimensions/format the system wants (usually a standard headshot JPEG; some portals give exact pixel sizes)
- Avoid filters — badge photos are for the security guard to compare to your face
- Plain, well-lit, recent; your ID should look like you today, not you on your best hair day of 2023
- Name the file sensibly before sending: `Doe_Jane_badge.jpg` — not `IMG_4921_final_FINAL(2).jpg` — keeps HR's day shorter and yours tidier
